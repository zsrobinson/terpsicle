# Terpsicle v3: technical plan for Plan and Todo

The plan every v3 agent builds from. It turns the owner's decisions of 2026-09-26 (evening) into names, tables, routes and an order of work for two new products: **Terpsicle Plan**, a four-year planner, and **Terpsicle Todo**, deadlines from ELMS. `docs/V2.md` is the model and the foundation: v3 builds on its identity, plan sync, push, PWA and route table, and doesn't change them except where this document says so.

**Precedence.** The owner's decisions win, then this document, then `docs/V2.md`, then the older docs. Once a table or schema lands in code, the code wins (`DATA.md`'s rule) and the PR updates this document too. Anything not decided here: pick the simplest option consistent with this file, `DESIGN.md` §5 and `SPEC.md` §3.13, and state the interpretation in the PR.

**When.** Build v3 after v2's core lands. The waves (§11) say which v2 PRs each v3 PR needs; nothing here is a reason to hold up a v2 PR.

**Out of scope for v3's first release** (each has a home in §9 or is simply cut):
- **Degree requirements.** Plan tracks credits, GenEd and prerequisites. It doesn't audit a major, and no major's quirks (CS areas, SOCY research courses) are modeled. Reading requirements from the catalog is a later phase (§9.1).
- A GPA panel, "what if" grades, own todos typed in Terpsicle, the "exam in a week" and "new on the feed" notifications, study-group cards in Chat, the review-ask email, PDF transcript upload, share links for a four-year plan.
- Earshot and Orgs stay dropped.

No product tab is selected on Home, and Feedback files as `site`.
- **The page:** the family bar, then the kit's `PageHeader` ("Good morning", "Good afternoon" or "Good evening" by New York's clock; the date, and "Week 5 of Fall 2026" when Now's academic calendar is published), then two columns from 1024px, split by time: **Now** takes two thirds, **Next** one third. Each part is a `PageSection` under its product's mark with its "View …" link; rows are `ListRow`s, course codes the course's tinted tag (the scheduler's color, as in Todo), the same color everywhere on the page.
  - **Now: Today** (Schedule): the day's classes in the main plan of the term in session: time, course, room, meeting kind, and before a class, the walk from the one before it ("Tight: 8 min walk from ESJ", the Travel tab's math and verdicts, never red). Between terms, or with no plan for Now: one quiet line.
  - **Now: This week** (Todo, signed in, with ELMS connected or anything on the list): Todo's week (Monday to Sunday, or from Sunday by Todo's setting), a class to a row (`weekByClass`): the class's next open deadline, with a checkbox that checks it off with Undo (`todo_item_checked`, `via: "home"`), when it's due ("Due in 3 hours" today), "+2 more this week", and "3 of 5 done" over a bar in the course's color.   - **Now: Chat** (signed in, while Chat is on, once any of your rooms has a message): your rooms with unread messages in Now's term (Next between terms), a course at a time with its count, from `chat/unread` (one D1 query that wakes no room); muted rooms don't count. Each row opens the course's room with the newest message.
  - **Next: the next term** (Schedule, titled "Spring 2027" with its Next tag): Next's main plan in a line ("7 courses · 16 credits · 2 registered", the credits as the scheduler counts them) with its problems (`planProblems`, watches as notes), and signed in, each watched section there with a seat open now. The row opens the plan's Problems tab.
  - **Next: Four-year plan** (Plan, while Plan is listed, when a four-year plan is on this device): "30 of 120 credits" over a bar, and "3 of 11 GenEds covered" (counting planned courses), counted as Plan counts them.
  - **Next: Coming up** (Todo): up to four open deadlines in the three weeks after this one (`comingUp`), Todo's own rows, checked off with Undo. Nothing coming: no section.
  - **Next: Review your instructors** (Reviews, signed in, while Reviews is `on`): up to three instructors from `instructorsToReview`, fed each term's main plan, less the ones you've reviewed (`reviews/mine`); each opens the review form. Nothing to ask: no section.
- **Setup callouts** (`chooseCallouts` in `src/core/home/callouts.ts`, `HomeCallout`): for a product you haven't set up, one quiet card (the kit's `Card`: the product's mark, what you'd see, one outline button, and Close) in the place its part would be. **Todo:** signed in with no ELMS calendar ("See this week's assignments here", Connect ELMS → `/todo/connect`). **Chat:** signed in, in a term, and none of your rooms has a message yet ("Every class has a chat room", Open Chat). **Next term:** no plan with courses for Next ("Plan Spring 2027" → `/schedule?term=`). **Plan:** no four-year plan ("Map out your four years" → `/plan`). **Signed out,** one callout for signing in stands for Todo and Chat ("Sign in for more", the Google button, `signin_started` with `from: "home"`). A product that's off gets none, and none shows until what's set up is known (so none flashes by). - **Fast, and still:** it reads this device first, raw from IndexedDB (plans, blocks, settings, four-year plans; no Dexie, no scheduler stores), so those parts show at once. Published files come after: Now and Next's academic calendars, the campus map only when Now has a plan, the next term's manifest, the plan's departments, seats and changes, and the course index's departments for Plan. In each column the device's parts come before the account's, and each part holds its skeleton's height while it loads, so nothing already shown moves. Signed in, the synced prefs follow the account as on Settings (plan sync, loaded only with a session).
- **Analytics:** `/home` is on the no-autocapture list (it shows Todo's titles and your courses). `home_link_clicked` (`to`: the product) says which parts get followed; `home_callout_followed` and `home_callout_dismissed` (`callout`: its id) say which callouts work. Never the course, room or item.
- **Budget:** `HOME_BUDGET` in `scripts/check-bundle.ts`, with Reviews' never-eager rules (no Dexie, no stores but the data reader; the published queries and the disk cache load on first use).

## 2. Terpsicle Plan

### 2.1 What the first release does

- Eight fall and spring semesters as columns, plus "Before UMD" (AP and transfer credit) and any summer or winter term that has a course. Courses are blocks: code, credits (or grade, once taken), title, GenEd chips. Add, move and remove, with undo.
- **Credits:** earned, in progress and planned, against 120.
- **GenEd progress** from Testudo's GenEd codes (§2.7).
- **Prerequisite problems as information** (§2.8). They never block anything.
- **Wildcards:** placeholder blocks like `CMSC4XX` and "Any DSHS course" (§2.9).
- **Transcript paste import** (§2.10).
- **Sample templates**, starting with Computer Science (§2.11).
- **"View schedule"** hands the next semester to the scheduler (§2.12).
- **Works signed out** in the browser, like the scheduler; signing in syncs it (§2.4).
- The sidebar says, always and quietly: "Terpsicle doesn't check your major's requirements. Your degree audit does." with a link to u.achieve. No audit, no requirement names.

### 2.2 The course index (R2 family `courses/`)

The scheduler's catalog is per term and covers only the terms Testudo lists (about four). A four-year plan needs every course, including ones not offered this term, so the catalog job publishes a **course index** across every term it has seen.

| Key | Schema | Written by | Named |
|---|---|---|---|
| `courses/manifest.json` | `CourseIndexManifestSchema` `{schemaVersion, generatedAt, search: {hash}, departments: [{code, hash}]}` | catalog job (6 h) | fixed |
| `courses/search.<hash>.json` | `CourseSearchFileSchema`: per course `[code, title, creditsMin, creditsMax, genEdCodes]`, sorted by code | catalog job | hashed |
| `courses/dept/<DEPT>.<hash>.json` | `CourseIndexDeptSchema`: `courses`, sorted by code, each `{code, title, credits, genEds, prerequisite, corequisite, restriction, crossListings, prereqs, offered}` | catalog job | hashed |

Landed in `v3/course-index`; `src/core/schema/course-index.ts` and `DATA.md` §3.4 and §5.2 are the contract now.

- **Source:** the newest term's department chunk that lists the course (active terms first, then archived ones still in R2). `offered` is the list of term ids the course appeared in, newest first, so the UI can say "Last offered Fall 2025" and "Usually offered in fall".
- `genEds` is the catalog's shape (`DATA.md` §3.2: groups that all apply, options within a group, with `condition`).
- `prereqs` is `parsePrerequisite(prerequisite)` (§2.8), computed in ingest so every client gets the same answer. A course with no prerequisite has `{groups: [], complete: true}`.
- The index only reaches back to the terms the catalog job has seen (nothing is backfilled), so `offered` and `not-offered-lately` get more useful each term.
- Sizes: the search file is about 5,000 rows (~120 KB gzipped). Department files load when a plan has a course from that department or a search result opens.
- `SCHEMA_VERSIONS` gains `courses`. Caching follows `dataCachePolicy` like `catalog/` (fixed manifest `no-cache` with a 1 h edge TTL, hashed files immutable). The manifest is rewritten only when the index changed. Mock mode builds the index from `src/fixtures` with the same code.
- The client reads it through the query cache (`src/state/query/course-index.ts`, `DATA.md` §5.2): a saved manifest checked once per page, hashed files read by key, mock and live apart.

### 2.3 The four-year doc (`src/core/schema/four-year.ts`)

Code names stay unambiguous: the scheduler already has "plans" (Plan A, Plan B). The four-year plan is **`FourYear`** in code (`src/core/four-year`, `src/features/four-year`, sync kind `four-year`), and **Plan** only in UI copy and the route.

```ts
export const FourYearTermSchema = z.union([TermIdSchema, z.literal("before")]); // "before" = AP and transfer

export const FourYearEntrySchema = z.discriminatedUnion("kind", [
  z.object({
    kind: z.literal("course"),
    id: LocalIdSchema,
    term: FourYearTermSchema,
    code: CourseCodeSchema,
    /** Only for variable-credit courses; otherwise the index's credits. */
    credits: z.number().min(0).max(20).nullable(),
    /** The option picked where Testudo says "or", per GenEd group index. */
    genEdChoices: z.record(z.string(), GenEdCodeSchema),
    source: z.enum(["typed", "transcript", "template"]),
    /** The transcript's own title and kind, shown under the catalog title. */
    transcript: z.object({ title: z.string().max(120), via: z.enum(["umd", "ap", "transfer", "exam"]) }).nullable(),
    /** Course info for a code the index doesn't know (§2.8); absent in older docs. */
    details: z.object({ title: z.string().max(120).nullable(), genEds: z.array(GenEdCodeSchema).max(8), countsAs: CourseCodeSchema.nullable().optional() }).nullable().optional(),
  }),
  z.object({
    kind: z.literal("wildcard"),
    id: LocalIdSchema,
    term: FourYearTermSchema,
    wildcard: WildcardSchema,           // from ~/core/wildcards (§2.9)
    credits: z.number().min(0).max(20), // default 3
    source: z.enum(["typed", "template"]),
  }),
  z.object({
    /** AP, exam or transfer credit with no UMD equivalent ("CHEM 1XX, 4 credits"). */
    kind: z.literal("credit"),
    id: LocalIdSchema,
    term: z.literal("before"),
    title: z.string().min(1).max(120),          // the transcript's, never changed
    credits: z.number().min(0).max(40),
    genEds: z.array(GenEdCodeSchema),
    source: z.literal("transcript"),
    via: z.enum(["ap", "transfer", "exam"]).optional(),            // absent in older docs
    equivalentPattern: z.string().regex(/^[A-Z]{4}[0-9X]{3}$/).nullable().optional(), // "CHEM1XX"
    /** "Counts as" (§2.10): absent until the person says; null says "no UMD course". */
    countsAs: CourseCodeSchema.nullable().optional(),
  }),
]);

export const FourYearDocSchema = z.object({
  id: LocalIdSchema,
  name: z.string().min(1).max(60),          // "My plan"; "(copy)" rules as plans
  firstTermId: TermIdSchema,                // the first fall shown
  /** Entries in column order: term, then position. */
  entries: z.array(FourYearEntrySchema).max(150),
  /** Private (§2.5). Keyed by entry id; only course entries have one. */
  grades: z.record(LocalIdSchema, GradeSchema),
  /** Where a template came from, for its credit line. */
  template: z.object({ id: z.string(), department: z.string(), year: z.string() }).nullable(),
  createdAt: IsoDateTimeSchema,
  updatedAt: IsoDateTimeSchema,
});
// GradeSchema (src/core/schema/transcript.ts): "A+" … "F", "XF", "P", "S", "I", "NG", "AUD"
// (W, NC and dropped lines aren't imported).
```

- **Several docs, one open.** People mostly keep one, but a copy ("what if I switch majors") is a natural thing to want, and ids cost nothing now. The name has a ▾ menu like a plan tab: Rename, Duplicate, Delete (Undo), New. The open one is `UiPrefs.activeFourYearId` (local, not synced).
- **Status is derived, never stored:** `termStatus(term, today, calendars)` in core says `done`, `in-progress` or `planned` from the academic calendar (`calendar/<term>.json`: in progress from `classesStart` until 14 days after `classesEnd`, for finals and grades; by the season's months when the calendar isn't published). "Before" is always done.
- **Local first:** Dexie gains a `fourYear` table (key `id`) in the Dexie version after v2's (`LOCAL_DB_VERSION` + 1, with an `upgrade()`). Rows are validated on read like plans; an invalid row is skipped and logged, never fatal.
- **Undo** is the scheduler's reducer pattern (`src/core/plans/history.ts`): `fourYearReducer` in `src/core/four-year/reducer.ts` with actions `add`, `move`, `remove`, `resolve-wildcard`, `set-choice`, `set-credits`, `set-details`, `set-credit` (§2.10), `apply-template`, `import`, `rename`, `remove-grades`; ⌘Z and the toast's Undo.
- **As built** (`v3/four-year-core`): the schema is `src/core/schema/four-year.ts`, imported by that path and **not** re-exported from `src/core/schema/index.ts`, so its zod objects stay out of every product's eager bundle. `FourYearTermSchema` is the transcript's `TranscriptTermSchema`. Entry ids are unique within a doc; `genEdChoices` keys are group indexes (`"0"`, `"1"`); `firstTermId` may be a spring for someone who started in spring. `fourYearReducer` works over `{docs}` and also has `create` ("My plan", then "My plan 2"), `duplicate` ("Copy of …", the plans' `copyName`) and `delete`, so the ▾ menu's actions undo too; `import` takes the terms to replace (the caller's done and in-progress ones) and never grows a doc past 150 entries. `termStatus` without a published calendar uses fixed season spans (spring Jan 25–May 31, summer Jun 1–Aug 20, fall Aug 21–Dec 31, winter Jan 1–24 of the next year). Columns (`fourYearColumns`) are "Before UMD", eight fall/spring semesters from `firstTermId`, and any other term with an entry. The index a doc is checked against is `FourYearCourses` (`courses` loaded so far, plus `loadedDepts`), so a course isn't called unknown before its department file loads.

### 2.4 Sync: a new doc kind (migration `0010_four_year_sync.sql`)

A four-year doc is a third sync doc kind, next to `plan` and `settings` (V2 §5), with the same rules: saved whole with its base rev (compare-and-swap), never merged field by field.

- **Why a new kind and not part of the settings doc:** it's a person's biggest document, edited in bursts; inside the settings doc every GenEd choice would conflict with a block edit on another device. As its own doc, a conflict keeps both copies, the rule people already know.
- **The migration** rebuilds `sync_docs`, because SQLite can't alter a `CHECK`:
  - `kind IN ('plan', 'settings', 'four-year')`;
  - `CHECK (kind IN ('plan', 'four-year') OR deleted = 0)` (four-year docs have tombstones too);
  - `term_id` stays NULL for four-year docs.

  It runs as create `sync_docs_new` → copy → drop → rename → recreate `sync_docs_since` and `sync_docs_tombstones`, with `PRAGMA defer_foreign_keys = on`. Nothing references `sync_docs`, so the rebuild is local to it. A worker test applies every migration in order and round-trips existing rows.
- **Schemas:** `SyncDocKindSchema` gains `"four-year"`; `SyncDocSchema`, `SyncPushDocSchema` gain the branch (body `FourYearDocSchema`, body id = doc id). Limits: the same 64 KiB body; at most `SYNC_MAX_FOUR_YEAR_DOCS` = 20 live four-year docs per user (the `too-many-plans` result covers it; the UI says "You have 20 four-year plans. Delete one to make another.").
- **Older tabs must not break.** A tab from before this deploy parses pull results with a union that doesn't know `four-year`. So the first PR that touches sync (§11, `v3/four-year-sync-api`) makes the client parse pulled docs one by one and **skip a doc of an unknown kind** (logged, not fatal) instead of failing the page. And because a skipping client still advances its cursor, the Dexie upgrade that adds `fourYear` **resets the sync cursor to 0** once, so an upgraded device pulls everything and sees the four-year docs it skipped. A push that carries a four-year doc is answered `unavailable` until `PLAN_ENABLED` is on, which is a later deploy.
- **Server:** nothing reads a four-year body beyond validating it. `chat_members` is derived from plan docs only. The admin panel never shows sync docs.
- **Conflicts and first sign-in** reuse `src/core/sync` unchanged in spirit: a conflicting four-year doc is kept both ways ("<name> (copy)"); first sign-in uploads every local four-year doc the account lacks, and an untouched empty "My plan" isn't uploaded to an account that already has one. The toast counts plans and four-year plans together: "Your 3 plans and your four-year plan are on your account now. Nothing was dropped." After a first sign-in on a device, Plan opens the account's four-year plan (the one changed most recently), not the one this device made, and when this device's was kept under a new name the toast says that one thing: "My plan from this device is saved as My plan (copy)", then "Your account already had one by that name. My plan from your account is open." Only which plan opens and the toast's words changed (QA round 1, P4); the union is as above.
- **Sign-out** "and remove plans from this device" also clears `fourYear`.
- **As built** (`v3/four-year-sync`):
  - **One engine, every kind.** The scheduler's engine (`src/features/sync/engine.ts`) carries four-year docs as a third `DocKey` (`four-year:<id>`), in the same `syncDocs` flags, pull cursor, Web Lock and pushes. `SyncedTables` gains `fourYear`, so sync storage reads and writes the `fourYear` table in the same transaction as the plans. The engine runs on whichever page is open with that page's store: the scheduler's workspace (four-year docs stay in IndexedDB there) or, on `/plan`, Plan's store (plans and settings stay in IndexedDB there). Plan loads it lazily once `/api/me` says someone is signed in (`src/features/four-year/sync.ts`).
  - **Conflicts:** `resolveFourYearConflict` is the plans' rule: the same work (name, first semester, entries, grades, template; timestamps don't count) takes the server's; an edit beats a delete; otherwise the server's stays and this device's becomes "<name> (copy)" at the end of the list, with the same toast as a plan's copy.
  - **First sign-in:** `firstSignInUnion` joins four-year docs like plans, with the whole list as one "term": a name clash with the account's gets "(copy)", an id the account holds differently is kept both ways, and an untouched doc (`isUntouchedFourYear`: "My plan" or "My plan 2", nothing in it) isn't uploaded when the account already has a four-year plan. Four-year docs are the person's own doing, so unlike the scheduler's auto-made plans, a new one is pushed right away.
  - **Undo on `/plan`:** the account's docs are applied to every step of the history (`rebaseFourYearHistory`), so undo never brings back what they replaced. Coming back to `/plan` in the same page reads the docs again, since the scheduler's engine may have changed them meanwhile.
  - **The cap:** a push past `SYNC_MAX_FOUR_YEAR_DOCS` toasts "You have 20 four-year plans" ("Delete one to make another. Until then, new ones stay on this device.") and the status reads "Account full" with that reason, until the doc changes.
  - **Pulled bodies** are checked against `FourYearDocSchema` on the device; one that doesn't read is skipped and logged by id only, never with its contents (§2.5), like invalid `fourYear` rows in IndexedDB.
  - **Plan's header** shows the scheduler's status words and icon ("Saved", "Saving…", …) instead of "Saved in this browser" while sync runs, and pressing it checks with the account.

### 2.5 Grades are private

Grades come in with the transcript (the import's "Keep grades" checkbox, on by default) and show on done blocks. They exist for the person looking at their own plan, and later for a GPA panel.

- **Where they live:** only in the four-year doc's `grades` map, in the browser and, when signed in, in that doc on our server so they're on your other devices. One map, so "Remove grades" (in the doc's ▾ menu, with Undo) is one action and easy to verify.
- **Never anywhere else:** not in share links (four-year plans have none), exports, analytics events or properties, logs, error messages, push payloads, email, the admin panel, Chat, Reviews or any server computation. The server stores the body and never parses `grades`.
- **Never shown to anyone else.** No feature shows one person's plan to another.
- **Not imported:** W, NC, dropped and in-progress lines (they have no grade); the import's check step says which lines it left out and why.
- **UI:** grades carry `data-private`, and session recordings are off on `/plan` (§6).
- **Honest copy** on the import: "Grades stay in your plan. If you're signed in, they sync to your other devices through Terpsicle's server. Nobody else can see them." and on `/privacy` (§5.3). Sync is plain server-side storage, not end-to-end encrypted (V2 §5.1), and the copy doesn't pretend otherwise.

### 2.6 Credits (`src/core/four-year/credits.ts`)

- A course counts its index credits (or the entry's `credits` for a variable-credit course); a wildcard its own `credits`; a `credit` entry its credits.
- **Earned** = done terms, excluding F; **in progress** = the current term; **planned** = later terms. "Before UMD" counts as earned.
- A course taken twice counts once, the later attempt, and raises a `repeated-course` problem unless the catalog marks it repeatable ("Repeatable to 6 credits" in `otherNotes`).
- The header reads "99 of 120 credits", with the tooltip "Most UMD degrees need 120 credits. Yours may need more; your degree audit has the number."
- Per column: courses and credits ("5 courses · 15 cr").
- **As built:** besides F, the grades that earn nothing are XF, I, NG and AUD (`NO_CREDIT_GRADES`). The index doesn't keep `otherNotes`, but Testudo runs "Repeatable to 6 credits" into the prerequisite or restriction sentence, which it does keep, so `isRepeatable` reads those. A course whose department file hasn't loaded counts 0 credits until it does (like the scheduler's `planCredits`).

### 2.7 GenEd progress (`src/core/four-year/gen-ed.ts`)

- **Categories and counts** are a small hand-kept constant, `GEN_ED_REQUIREMENTS`, with its source (UMD's GenEd checklist) in a comment. They change rarely. First release:

  | Code | Label | Needed |
  |---|---|---|
  | FSAW, FSPW, FSOC, FSMA, FSAR | Fundamental Studies | 1 each |
  | DSNL, DSNS | Natural Sciences | 2, at least one DSNL (lab) |
  | DSHS | History and Social Sciences | 2 |
  | DSHU | Humanities | 2 |
  | DSSP | Scholarship in Practice | 2 |
  | SCIS | I-Series | 2 |
  | DVUP, DVCC | Diversity | 2, at least one DVUP |

- **Which course counts** comes from the course's `genEds` (DATA.md §3.2): every group applies; within a group the course counts for one option. Where a group has options, the entry's `genEdChoices` wins; otherwise `allocateGenEds` picks, pure and deterministic: done courses first, then in term order, each open option going to the category that's furthest from done. A choice the person made is never overridden.
- **Conditions:** "DSNL (if taken with GEOL110)" counts as DSNL only when GEOL110 is in the same or an earlier semester; otherwise the chip shows the condition and the course counts as DSNS if that's its other option.
- I-Series and Diversity count alongside a Distributive Studies code, as Testudo's groups already say.
- **As built** (`allocateGenEds`): choices and one-option groups are placed first, then open groups in the stated order, each to the option with the largest shortfall (a category's missing courses plus, for DSNL and DVUP, its "at least one"); ties go to Testudo's first option. A course counts once per category (AAAS100's DVUP and DVCC give Diversity one course). A chosen option whose condition doesn't hold stays chosen and shows its condition, but doesn't count. A condition that names no course can't be checked, so it doesn't hold. A course that earned nothing, or an attempt a retake replaced, counts for nothing. Resolving a GenEd placeholder with a course whose group offers that code sets the choice (`choicesForWildcard`). The requirement counts sum to 17 courses (5 Fundamental, 8 Distributive, 2 I-Series, 2 Diversity).
- **The GenEd view** (`/plan`): one row per category with done, planned and still needed ("2 done · 1 planned · of 2"), and for a category still short, "Find a course" → `/plan/search?gened=…`. The footer: "From Testudo's GenEd codes. Your degree audit is the official check."

### 2.8 Prerequisite problems (`src/core/four-year/prereqs.ts`, `problems.ts`)

Prerequisites are information. Nothing blocks a move, and nothing turns red (DESIGN §5).

- **Parsing** (`parsePrerequisite(text)` in `src/core/catalog/prereqs.ts`, run in ingest): Testudo's sentence is free text. The parser pulls course codes (`[A-Z]{4}\d{3}[A-Z]?`, and "CMSC 131" with a space) and splits clauses on `;` and ` and ` into groups that all apply, with ` or ` inside a group: `{groups: CourseCode[][], complete: boolean}`. A clause starting with "or" is an alternative to everything before it, so "GEOG276; or CMSC330 and CMSC351" becomes `[[GEOG276, CMSC330], [GEOG276, CMSC351]]`. What isn't a course never adds a requirement and never meets one ("A or permission" asks for A; "A and any STAT400-level course" asks only for A). `complete` is false whenever the sentence has anything else: "permission of", "or equivalent", scores, "must have completed", program names. The exact rules are `DATA.md` §3.4. **Golden tests** run it over every prerequisite sentence in the saved terms' fixtures and snapshot the results, so a change shows up in review.
- **The check:** each group needs one of its codes (or a cross-listed equivalent) in an earlier semester, "Before UMD" and the current term included. A course's own term never counts. Wildcards satisfy nothing, and `credit` entries only the course they count as (§2.10). A course with course info that counts as another meets what that course would.
- **Problem kinds** (`FourYearProblem`, same `MessagePart` shape as the scheduler's `Problem`, DATA.md §9):

  | Kind | Severity | When | Example |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | `prereq-order` | info | a group isn't met by an earlier semester | "CMSC351 is before CMSC250" |
  | `light-semester` | info | a planned fall or spring has 1–11 credits | "Fall 2028 has 6 credits. Full time is 12." |
  | `repeated-course` | info | the same course twice, not repeatable | "CMSC216 is in Fall 2026 and Spring 2027" |
  | `unknown-course` | warning | a code the index doesn't know, with no course info | "HIST999 isn't in Testudo" |
  | `not-offered-lately` | info | not in any of the last four fall/spring terms | "ARTT489 was last offered Spring 2024" |
  | `unmatched-credit` | info | transfer credit Testudo gave as a level ("CHEM 1XX"), and nobody has said what it counts as | "AP CHEMISTRY came in as CHEM 1XX" |

- **Every prerequisite problem shows Testudo's sentence** ("Testudo says: Minimum grade of C- in CMSC250 and CMSC216."), because the parser can't read everything and grade minimums aren't checked.
- **Fixes** follow the scheduler's rule: offered only when they create no new problem. `prereq-order` offers "Move CMSC351 to Spring 2027" (the first semester where every group is met); `repeated-course` offers "Remove the later one". Undo applies.
- A block with a problem gets the thin warning inset the prototype shows, never a red outline; the problem and its fix live in the Problems view.
- **As built** (`detectFourYearProblems`, schemas `FourYearProblemSchema` with subjects `entry` or `term` and fixes `move` or `remove`): prerequisites are checked for in-progress and planned courses only (a done semester is history, and "Before UMD" has no order); one `prereq-order` per course, titled "CMSC351 is before CMSC250" / "…is in the same semester as…" when one course is out of order, else "CMSC351 needs CMSC250 and CMSC216 first". A course that earned nothing meets no prerequisite. `repeated-course` isn't raised for a retake of a course that earned nothing, and offers no fix when the later attempt is done. `light-semester` counts placeholders' credits and skips empty semesters. `not-offered-lately` needs the newest term Testudo lists (`latestTermId`) and is checked only for planned courses. `unknown-course` waits for the department file. Warnings sort first, then the order core emits.
- **Course info** (`v3/plan-course-details`, owner, 2026-09-27: "if something's not recognized we should be able to provide more information about the course in order to resolve the problems, like specifying the gen-ed's it was meant to cover"). Testudo drops courses: honors seminars rotate on a two-year schedule, topics courses come and go, and the index only knows terms the catalog job has seen (`DATA.md` §3.4). A course entry for a code the index doesn't know can carry `details: {title, genEds}` (`FourYearCourseDetailsSchema`, optional so older docs parse), set for every entry of the code at once by `set-details` (credits too, when given; null clears; Undo takes it back). Each GenEd applies, as a one-option group in `allocateGenEds`; the title shows on the block and in the drill-in. Details answer `unknown-course`, and they're ignored once the index lists the code (`courseDetails`), so Testudo wins.
  - **Where people give it:** the course's drill-in has a "Course info" form (title, prefilled from the transcript; credits; GenEd toggles in the checklist's order). The Problems row and the block's menu both open it ("Add course info" / "Edit course info").
  - **An honors code** whose base course the index has (MATH241H → MATH241, `honorsBase`) gets a fix, "Count it as MATH241", that copies the base course's title, credits and one-option GenEd groups (where Testudo says "or", the person picks; the form's "Use MATH241's info" fills the same).
  - **The transcript import** keeps the GenEds a transcript line prints for a code the index doesn't have as its course info, so a re-imported honors seminar counts without asking (`buildTranscriptImport`). Only a code the transcript names: a mapping someone typed could be off. Course info outlives a later import of the same code.
  - **Counts as** (`v3/transfer-credits`): course info can also name the UMD course the code counts as (`details.countsAs`), picked from Plan's course search. It then meets prerequisites and repeats like that course (`countsAsCode` in `course-lookup.ts`, used by `prereqs.ts`, `supersededAttempts` and `repeated-course`), while the block keeps its own title with "Counts as MATH241" under it. The honors fix "Count it as MATH241" sets it too. The form's "Use MATH241's credits and GenEds" fills the rest from the course.
- **A course is never its own prerequisite** (`v3/templates`): Testudo's MATH140 reads "Minimum grade of C- in MATH115; or must have math eligibility of MATH140 or higher", which the parser reads as the group MATH115 or MATH140. The four-year checks drop a course's own code from its groups (and a group that was only that code), so MATH140 in a first semester says "MATH140 needs MATH115 first", as information, by the rule that "A or something uncheckable" still asks for A.

### 2.9 Wildcards (the shared matcher from `v2/wildcards`)

The scheduler's Generate tab and Plan share one matcher in core, landed in **`v2/wildcards`** as `src/core/catalog/wildcard.ts` (schemas in `src/core/schema/wildcard.ts`). Plan uses it and adds nothing to the matching:

```ts
WildcardSchema = z.discriminatedUnion("kind", [
  z.object({ kind: z.literal("pattern"), pattern: WildcardPatternSchema }), // "CMSC4XX", "CMSC42X", "ARTTXXX"
  z.object({ kind: z.literal("gen-ed"), code: GenEdCodeSchema }),           // any DSHS course
]);
parseWildcard(input: string): WildcardParse;  // {kind: "wildcard", wildcard} | {kind: "invalid", message} | {kind: "none"}
matchesWildcard(wildcard: Wildcard, course: {code, genEds}, {conditional?}): boolean;
wildcardLabel(w: Wildcard): string;           // "Any CMSC 400-level", "Any DSHS course"
wildcardId(w: Wildcard): WildcardId;          // "CMSC4XX", "gen-ed:DSHS"
```

Plan's side is `src/core/four-year/wildcards.ts`: `parsePlaceholder` (the matcher's parse, plus a leading "any"), `resolvesWildcard` (the matcher with its defaults: a conditional GenEd doesn't resolve a placeholder, a choice between codes resolves either), `searchRowMayResolve` (a quick pass over the search file before department files load) and `choicesForWildcard`. Placeholder credits are `WILDCARD_CREDITS` (3, 1–6).

In Plan:
- **Adding:** typing `CMSC4XX` or "any DSHS" in Plan's search offers "Add a placeholder: CMSC4XX" as the first result. A template can include them.
- **A placeholder block** is dashed, shows its label and credits (default 3, editable 1–6), and counts toward credits. A GenEd wildcard counts as *planned* for its category; a pattern wildcard counts toward no category. Placeholders satisfy no prerequisite and have no prerequisites.
- **Resolving:** "Pick a course" opens `/plan/search?wildcard=<id>` with the matcher as a filter; choosing one replaces the block in place (Undo).
- **Handoff:** placeholders can't become scheduler bookmarks; §2.12 says what happens.

### 2.10 Transcript import

**A pure core parser with golden tests** (`src/core/four-year/transcript/parse.ts`); the UI is the Import view.

```ts
parseTranscript(text: string): TranscriptParse;   // schemas in src/core/schema/transcript.ts
// TranscriptParse = {
//   lines: TranscriptLine[],     // what we read, in order
//   skipped: { raw: string, reason: "withdrawn" | "dropped" | "no-credit" | "unreadable",
//              line: TranscriptLine | null }[],   // what it would import as (null: unreadable)
//   recognized: boolean,         // false: this doesn't look like a UMD unofficial transcript
// }
// TranscriptLine = { term: TermId | "before", code: CourseCode | null, title: string,
//   grade: Grade | null, credits: number, earned: number | null, qualityPoints: number | null,
//   genEds: GenEdGroup[], via: "umd" | "ap" | "transfer", equivalentOf: CourseCode | null,
//   equivalentPattern: string | null, sectionCode: SectionCode | null, inProgress: boolean }
```

- **As built** (`v3/transcript-parser`): `code` is the UMD course a line counts as, so for AP and transfer lines it equals `equivalentOf`, and it's null when the credit has no single UMD course. A generic equivalent (`CHEM 1XX`) is kept as `equivalentPattern` for the check step's transfer mapping. `skipped[].line` is what the check step ticks back in. Columns are told apart by gaps of two or more spaces (or a tab), which is how "VITAMIN D" stays a title and `INTRO   D   3.00` reads as a drop; indentation isn't needed. The golden pastes are synthetic until owner action 1 (`src/core/four-year/transcript/__fixtures__/README.md` says how a redacted real paste replaces them).

- **The format**, from v1's `lib/transcript-parser.ts` (terpsicle-bitcamp, 2025): everything before the first line containing `@` is header and is ignored; transfer and AP lines come first, with a grade of `P`, `NC` or a letter, credits like `3.00`, the UMD equivalent's code and GenEd codes after it; then term lines (`Fall 2025`); course lines are indented at least four spaces: code, title, grade, credits attempted, earned, quality points, GenEd codes; in-progress terms carry a four-digit section code as the second token and no grade; a `D` marks a dropped course; lines starting with `Meth` or `=` are page furniture. GenEd lists split on `,` into groups and on ` or ` into options, the same shape as the catalog's.
- **The first job is a fresh paste** (owner action, §10): the layout may have moved since 2025. v1's parser is a guide, not a spec.
- **Golden tests** on real pastes in `src/core/four-year/transcript/__fixtures__/`, redacted before they're committed: name, UID, birth date, address and email replaced with fixed fakes, and a test asserts the fixtures contain none of the original values' shapes (a 9-digit UID, an email other than the fake). At least: a plain four-semester paste, one with AP and transfer credit, one with a W, a drop and an in-progress term. `parseTranscript` throws on nothing: bad input gives `recognized: false`.
- **Header data never leaves the parser.** Names, UIDs and addresses aren't in `TranscriptParse`. The paste lives in component state only: never stored, never sent, never logged, never in the URL, never in analytics or error reports.
- **Matching:** each line's code is looked up in the course index. The catalog title shows with the transcript title under it. A transfer line with no equivalent becomes a `credit` entry. A code the index doesn't know stays a course entry; with the GenEds its line prints as course info (§2.8), else with an `unknown-course` problem.

- **Transfer and exam credit, as built** (`v3/transfer-credits`, owner, 2026-09-28: "we should have a better experience for transfer credits from other universities … it should be easy to override things about courses it can't find the match for from Testudo, like the course it should count towards, the gen-eds it should count for, etc."). The golden pastes are still synthetic (`synthetic-transfer-credit.txt` covers what follows), shaped by the registrar's published evaluation codes, until a real paste arrives.
  - **Kinds:** AP (a title starting "AP", or under an Advanced Placement heading), exam (IB, CLEP, DSST, credit by exam: `via: "exam"`) and transfer (anything under a school's heading). Several schools in a row are fine; a school's name is a heading, never a course.
  - **What a line may hold** after its title: a grade (a letter, P, NC, W, or CR/T/TR), or none at all; the UMD equivalent (`MATH140`, `CHEM 1XX`, or an elective that names no course: `LTR`, `UTR`, `XXX 1XX`); the credits; GenEds; and the registrar's evaluation codes (L1, L2, G1, G2, N1, N2, R1, R2, LB) and footnotes (two letters or digits), which aren't GenEds and are dropped. NE, ST and UR mean UMD hasn't evaluated it: the line is left out as `not-evaluated`, and can be ticked back in. An AP score ("SCR 4") and the other school's own code after a slash ("…/SOCY 101") come off the title.
  - **Wrapped rows** are joined back on: a title that ran onto the next row (a row of capitals with no columns, followed by one that reads as credit), a UMD course's title that did, and GenEds after a trailing "," or "or".
  - **Where it's listed:** credit under a term heading it was accepted in ("Fall 2023", before any UMD course of that term) and a transfer section after the terms both land in Before UMD.
  - **In the plan:** a line naming a UMD course is that course, as before. Anything else is a `credit` entry that keeps the transcript's title, credits and GenEds, with `via` and `equivalentPattern`, in Before UMD, counted in credits and GenEds like any done course. The block reads "AP credit · CHEM 1XX" (or "Transfer credit", "Exam credit"), and opens a drill-in (`?credit=<entry id>`, like `?course=`) with "What it counts as": Counts as (Plan's course search, the pattern's courses first), credits and GenEds, saved by one `set-credit` action (Undo). `countsAs` is absent until the person says; null says "no UMD course". The check step's mapping field sets the same `countsAs` (only a code Testudo lists) rather than making a course entry. What someone said about a credit (matched by title and placeholder) outlives a re-import, like course info.
  - **Problems:** `unmatched-credit` (info) asks what a "CHEM 1XX" credit counts as, with "Choose what it counts as" opening its drill-in; saying a course or none answers it. Credit that counts as a course repeats it: take the course at UMD and the credit stops counting, with `repeated-course`.
- **What an import keeps** (checked for `v3/transfer-credits`): the paste lives in `import-state.ts`'s memory-only store and in the textarea (marked `data-private`, so feedback screenshots black it out); nothing writes it to IndexedDB, the URL, a server function, sync, analytics (`transcript_parsed` sends counts and a boolean), the feedback activity log or a log line. The parser's output has no header field. Saved per imported entry: a course's code, `transcript: {title, via}`, term, credits (only where the index would count differently) and "or" choices, and for a code the index doesn't know, the GenEds its line prints as course info; a credit's title, credits, GenEds, `via` and `equivalentPattern`; and grades in the doc's `grades` map when "Keep grades" is on. Never kept: quality points, earned credits, section codes, the schools' names, the term credit was accepted in, evaluation codes, skipped and unreadable lines. The Import view says so: "Your transcript is read here, in your browser, and never saved or sent. Only the courses you import are saved."

**The Import view** (`/plan/import`): three steps, Paste → Check → Import.
1. **Paste.** "In Testudo, open Unofficial Transcript, select everything on the page (⌘A), copy, and paste it here. Nothing leaves your browser until you import." Parsing runs as you paste.
2. **Check.** What we read, by semester, live next to the paste on desktop and as a list on the phone. Three fix-ups only: choose the GenEd where Testudo says "or" (the Import button waits until each is chosen), tick a skipped line back in, and map a transfer course we couldn't match. "Keep grades" checkbox, on.
3. **Import.** One `import` action: it replaces the doc's done and in-progress terms with what was read (typed entries in those terms are kept only if the transcript doesn't have them) and leaves planned terms alone. Undo in the toast. If the plan is empty, `firstTermId` becomes the transcript's first fall or spring. The toast: "Imported 16 courses from 4 semesters."
- `recognized: false` gets a specific line: "That doesn't look like a UMD unofficial transcript. Copy the whole page from Testudo's Unofficial Transcript."
- Import works signed out.
- **As built** (`v3/transcript-import`): the check step's pure side is `src/core/four-year/transcript/import.ts` (`transcriptRows`, `pendingChoices`, `buildTranscriptImport`, `importReplaceTerms`, `importSummary`); the UI is `src/features/four-year/import-panel.tsx`. The paste is in the sidebar's Import view; the check list sits in the board's place beside it on desktop (from 769px, since `v3/plan-workbench`) and under the paste on a phone. The paste and every decision about it live in a memory-only zustand store (`import-state.ts`), shared by the two, and are dropped when the import is done or you leave the Import view or Plan (not when a resize moves the panel between layouts). While checking, titles and "not in Testudo" come from the search file every visit loads anyway, so nothing about the paste is fetched; department files load when Import is pressed. Skipped lines (W, dropped, NC) are listed unticked and import without a grade when ticked; unreadable lines are listed as pasted. AP and transfer credit without a UMD course gets a mapping field (suggestions from its `CHEM 1XX` pattern); left empty it's a `credit` entry titled "AP CHEMISTRY (CHEM 1XX)", and its GenEds are the options picked. A course entry keeps the transcript's credits only where the index would count something else (variable credit, AP credit worth less than the course, a code the index lacks); its "or" choice is stored on the catalog's group index. The empty state's "Import your transcript" opens a new plan on `/plan/import`, and the import then sets `firstTermId`. The toast reads "Imported 21 courses from 4 semesters and Before UMD" (no period, like every Plan toast). On a phone the strip then opens on the latest semester that came in (`latestTermOf`), not Now's, which is likely still empty. ## 3. Terpsicle Todo

### 3.1 What the first release does

- **Connect ELMS** by pasting the ELMS calendar feed link (an `.ics` URL). One paste, no password, no approval from UMD.
- **A week** (§3.9, since `v3/todo-calendar`; the week alone since `v3/todo-week`), with checkboxes; a sidebar to add tasks in plain words, see each course's week and connect ELMS.
- **One notification**, "Due tomorrow" (§4).
- **A calendar file** can stand in for ELMS or add to it (§3.7).
- **No exam marking and no Gradescope detection** (the owner, 2026-09-28; `docs/decisions.md`): an item is what ELMS says it is.
- **Signed in only.** Not in the first release: "exam in a week", "new on the feed", marking done in ELMS. Own todos were on this list too; the owner asked for them on 2026-09-27, and they're §3.10.

**Why the calendar feed.** The prototype compared three ways in. The feed needs no approval and covers dated assignments, quizzes and calendar events for every course. A personal access token would put the student's whole ELMS in our database and may be disabled for students; LTI or a developer key needs DIT's approval, which a student project won't get. The feed's known limits (reported by campus IT pages): about 1,000 items, a year ahead and 30 days back, no undated work, no ELMS to-do items, no grades. Copy says what does and doesn't come through: "Assignments, quizzes and exams with a due date, and events your professors put on the ELMS calendar. Undated work and grades don't come through."

### 3.2 Connecting (`todo/connect`)

**The link is a secret:** anyone with it can read that student's due dates. It's handled like a password from the moment it's pasted.

1. **Shape check, in the browser and again on the server** (`parseFeedLink` in `src/core/todo/link.ts`, pure): after trimming, it must be exactly `https://<host>/feeds/calendars/user_<token>.ics` with `<host>` one of `elms.umd.edu` and `umd.instructure.com`, and `<token>` 20–80 characters of `[A-Za-z0-9]`. No other host, port, path, query, fragment or credentials. The answer to a bad link never echoes it: "That isn't an ELMS calendar link. In ELMS, open Calendar, click Calendar Feed, and copy the link." (`webcal://` is accepted and rewritten to `https://`, since some browsers copy that.) **As built** (`fix/elms-connect-reasons`): a link on `elms.umd.edu` is moved to the same path on `umd.instructure.com`, since `elms.umd.edu` is UMD's landing page and answers 404 for every feed; only Canvas's host is fetched and stored.
2. **One fetch before storing** (§3.5's fetcher, with 25 seconds rather than 10, since the person is waiting and Canvas builds a whole feed as it's asked): it must answer 200 with a body that parses as a calendar. Failures answer `unreachable` or `not-a-calendar` with a `reason` code, and the form says one sentence for it (`connectWords` in `src/core/todo/connect.ts`): "ELMS took too long to send your calendar. Try again in a minute." for `timeout`, "ELMS doesn't know that link anymore; it changes if you reset it. Copy it again from Calendar Feed." for a 404 or 410, and so on. Nothing is stored on failure.
3. **Store it encrypted** (§3.3), upsert the `todo_feeds` row, write the items, and answer `{status: "connected", items, feed}`. Reconnecting replaces the link and keeps done marks for items that come back with the same UID.

**The connect page** (`/todo/connect`): three numbered steps ("Open elms.umd.edu and go to Calendar." / "Click Calendar Feed at the bottom right and copy the link." / "Paste it here.") and the note: "This link is a secret: anyone with it can read your due dates. We keep it encrypted and fetch it from our server, so reminders work when the app's closed. Disconnect any time." The input is `type="url"`, `autocomplete="off"`, `spellcheck="false"`, `data-private`, and `ph-no-capture`; the value is sent only in the `todo/connect` POST body and cleared from state after the answer.

**Disconnect** (`todo/disconnect`): no dialog. The UI shows the disconnected state at once with Undo in the toast and calls the route once the Undo toast is gone (the app's 10-second window, held while Undo has focus; the pattern `reviews/delete` uses). The route deletes the feed row, its items and done marks in one batch.

### 3.3 Encrypting the link

- **AES-256-GCM** with WebCrypto, key from the Worker secret **`TODO_FEED_KEY`** (32 random bytes, base64url), imported once per isolate as a non-extractable key.
- **Stored as one string** in `todo_feeds.url_enc`: `v1.<keyId>.<iv>.<ciphertext>` (base64url; a fresh 12-byte IV each time). `keyId` is the var `TODO_FEED_KEY_ID` (`"k1"` at first).
- **Bound to its row:** the additional authenticated data is `todo-feed:<userId>:<source>`, so a ciphertext copied to another user's row won't decrypt.
- **Rotation:** set the new key as `TODO_FEED_KEY` with a new `TODO_FEED_KEY_ID`, and the old one as `TODO_FEED_KEY_PREVIOUS` (with `TODO_FEED_KEY_PREVIOUS_ID`). Reads try the key whose id the row names; the fetch cron re-encrypts rows under the current key as it touches them; once none name the old id, remove `TODO_FEED_KEY_PREVIOUS`.
- `src/server/todo/crypto.ts` has the only code that decrypts, and it hands the URL only to the fetcher. No other function, route or log sees it.
- Missing key (previews, `pnpm dev:mock`): `todo/*` answers `unavailable` except in test mode, where `pnpm dev:mock` and e2e use a fixed test key and a fixture feed server (§11, `v3/todo-e2e`).

### 3.4 D1 (migration `0011_todo.sql`)

```sql
-- A connected source per user. Only 'elms' is fetched; the key leaves room for
-- another source without a rebuild.
CREATE TABLE todo_feeds (
  user_id          TEXT NOT NULL REFERENCES users (id) ON DELETE CASCADE,
  source           TEXT NOT NULL CHECK (source IN ('elms')),
  url_enc          TEXT NOT NULL,             -- v1.<keyId>.<iv>.<ciphertext>; never logged, never returned
  status           TEXT NOT NULL CHECK (status IN ('active', 'paused', 'broken')),
  created_at       TEXT NOT NULL,
  next_fetch_at    TEXT NOT NULL,
  last_fetch_at    TEXT,
  last_success_at  TEXT,
  failure_count    INTEGER NOT NULL DEFAULT 0, -- consecutive
  last_error       TEXT,                       -- a code: 'timeout', 'http-404', 'not-a-calendar', …
  gone_strikes     INTEGER NOT NULL DEFAULT 0, -- 401/403/404/410 in a row, at least an hour apart; 3 → broken
  gone_at          TEXT,                       -- when the last strike counted
  etag             TEXT,
  last_modified    TEXT,
  content_hash     TEXT,                       -- 16 hex of SHA-256 of the body: unchanged feeds write nothing
  item_count       INTEGER NOT NULL DEFAULT 0,
  last_opened_at   TEXT NOT NULL,              -- the person last loaded /todo (for the cadence)
  PRIMARY KEY (user_id, source)
);
CREATE INDEX todo_feeds_due ON todo_feeds (status, next_fetch_at);

-- Items from the feed or a dropped file. Rewritten by each fetch.
CREATE TABLE todo_items (
  user_id       TEXT NOT NULL REFERENCES users (id) ON DELETE CASCADE,
  uid           TEXT NOT NULL,                -- the ICS UID (≤ 200 chars)
  source        TEXT NOT NULL CHECK (source IN ('elms', 'file')),
  title         TEXT NOT NULL,                -- SUMMARY without the course bracket, ≤ 300 chars
  course_label  TEXT,                         -- the bracket's text: "CMSC216-0103: Introduction to Computer Systems"
  course_code   TEXT,                         -- matched from course_label (§3.6), or null
  section_code  TEXT,
  kind          TEXT NOT NULL CHECK (kind IN ('assignment', 'event')),
  due_at        TEXT,                         -- instant; null for an all-day item
  due_date      TEXT NOT NULL,                -- America/New_York date of due_at, or the all-day date
  link          TEXT,                         -- an ELMS URL from the item, same hosts as §3.2, or null
  first_seen_at TEXT NOT NULL,
  updated_at    TEXT NOT NULL,
  PRIMARY KEY (user_id, uid)
);
CREATE INDEX todo_items_by_date ON todo_items (user_id, due_date);
CREATE INDEX todo_items_due_day ON todo_items (due_date, user_id);

-- Done marks, kept apart from items so a refetch never clears them.
CREATE TABLE todo_done (
  user_id  TEXT NOT NULL REFERENCES users (id) ON DELETE CASCADE,
  uid      TEXT NOT NULL,
  done_at  TEXT NOT NULL,
  PRIMARY KEY (user_id, uid)
);
```

- Rows are validated on read with `TodoFeedRowSchema`, `TodoItemRowSchema` (`src/server/todo/store.ts`), like `alerts/store.ts`. `TodoFeedRowSchema` never includes `url_enc` in anything it returns to a handler; only `crypto.ts` reads that column.
- **Window:** only items with `due_date` from 30 days ago to 365 days ahead are kept; at most 1,500 per user. Descriptions are never stored (they're long, may be HTML, and we don't show them).
- **Pruning** (the daily job): items with `due_date` more than 30 days ago, and `todo_done` rows whose item has been gone for 30 days.
- **Account deletion** cascades every table. Disconnect deletes at once (§3.2).
- **Own tasks** (§3.10) are a fourth table, `todo_tasks` (`0015_todo_tasks.sql`), and their done marks live in `todo_done` like any item's.
- **Hidden courses** (§3.11) are `todo_hidden` (`0016_todo_hidden.sql`), kept next to done marks. Disconnecting keeps them: they're a preference.
- **`exam` and `gradescope` are gone** (`0022_drop_unused_columns`): `v3/todo-calendar` stopped reading and writing them but left them in place, because migrations run before the deploy (and on the shared preview database), so dropping them in the same PR would have broken writes from the build still serving. Once it was deployed, a later migration dropped them.
- **As built** (`v3/todo-api`): `gone_strikes` and `gone_at` were added so "three in a row, at least an hour apart" survives refreshes in between (a gone answer within the hour counts once; any other failure starts over). `exam` and `gradescope` gained `CHECK (… IN (0, 1))` (both dropped since). The cron treated "Due tomorrow" as off until `v3/todo-notify` (§4 "As built"), which reads `todoDue` for the cadence; the `notification_deliveries` CHECK landed with `0006_notifications`. Pruning's "gone for 30 days" uses the done mark's own age, since nothing records when an item left the feed.

### 3.5 Fetching: cadence and backoff (`src/jobs/todo-feeds.ts`, `src/server/todo/fetch.ts`)

**A cron every 20 minutes** at `"3,23,43 * * * *"` (off the seats job's minutes). Each run takes the feeds with `status = 'active'` and `next_fetch_at <= now`, oldest first, up to `TODO_FETCH_BATCH` (400), 8 at a time.

**The fetcher** (the only code that fetches a feed):
- `GET` the decrypted URL with `redirect: "manual"`; follow at most 2 redirects, each re-checked with `parseFeedLink`'s host rule; anything else is `bad-redirect`.
- 10 s timeout (25 s for `todo/connect`'s first fetch, §3.2); a body over 5 MB is cut off and counts as `too-large`.
- Sends `If-None-Match` / `If-Modified-Since` when we have them; a 304 or an unchanged `content_hash` updates only the feed row's timestamps.
- **Errors become codes**, never messages: `fetch()` errors and `Response` objects can carry the URL, so the fetcher catches everything and returns `{ok: false, code}` with a code from a fixed list (`timeout`, `network`, `http-<status>`, `too-large`, `not-a-calendar`, `bad-redirect`). Nothing from the error object is kept.

**Cadence:**

| Feed | Next fetch |
|---|---|
| Opened `/todo` in the last 14 days, or "Due tomorrow" on | 20 min |
| Otherwise | 6 h |
| Not opened in 120 days and "Due tomorrow" off | `paused`: not fetched. Opening `/todo` resumes it and fetches at once. |

**Backoff:** after a failure, `next_fetch_at = now + min(20 min × 2^(failures − 1), 12 h)`, with ±10% jitter so failures don't bunch. `http-401`, `http-403`, `http-404` and `http-410` mean ELMS stopped sharing that link (a reset feed or a removed account): three in a row, at least an hour apart, set `status = 'broken'` and stop fetching. `/todo` then says, in place and without a banner: "ELMS stopped sharing your calendar. Paste a new link." Other failures never break a feed; the list shows "ELMS feed checked 3 hours ago" so stale data is visible.

**On demand:** `todo/refresh` (the list's refresh control, and on opening `/todo` when the last success is over 10 minutes old) fetches that one feed now, at most once per 5 minutes per user.

**Writing:** parse (§3.6), then one D1 batch per feed: upsert items that changed, delete items no longer in the feed (their done marks stay until pruning), update the feed row. Unchanged feeds write one row.

**Scale:** at 400 feeds per run and three runs an hour, about 8,000 active feeds keep a 20-minute cadence; beyond that the cadence stretches and nothing is dropped. The admin health header (V2 §10) shows active, paused and broken feeds and the last run. Moving fetching to a Queue is a later PR if it's ever needed.

### 3.6 Parsing the feed and matching courses (`src/core/todo/`, pure)

- **`parseIcs(text)`**: a small RFC 5545 subset, no library: unfold lines, read `VEVENT`s' `UID`, `SUMMARY`, `DTSTART` (a `DATE`, a UTC `DATE-TIME`, or one with `TZID`), `DTEND` and `URL` (never `DESCRIPTION`, since `v3/todo-calendar`); unescape text. Anything unreadable is skipped and counted, never fatal. `recognized` is false when there's no `BEGIN:VCALENDAR`.
- **Kind:** `assignment` when the UID names one (`event-assignment-…`), else `event`. The due time is `DTSTART` (Canvas puts an assignment's due time there).
- **No exam guess** (`v3/todo-calendar`): the feed doesn't say which items are exams, and a keyword guess ("Final Project Proposal") was wrong often enough to mislead, so Todo shows every item the same way.
- **Course matching:** Canvas titles end with the course in brackets: `Project 2 [CMSC216-0103: Introduction to Computer Systems]`. `splitFeedTitle(summary)` takes the last `[...]` as `course_label` and the rest as `title`. `matchFeedCourse(label)` finds `[A-Z]{4}\d{3}[A-Z]?`, optionally followed by `-` and a four-character section; with several codes (merged sections, cross-listings like `CMSC216/ENEE…`), all are returned in order. The server stores the first; the client re-matches against the person's main plans with `pickFeedCourse(codes, planCourses)` (the first code in a main plan, else the first code; drafts don't count, V2 §5.5) to color the item with that course's scheduler color. An item with no code keeps its ELMS course name and a neutral color.
- **Which term:** `termForDate(date, calendars)` picks the term whose calendar span (classes through 14 days after) contains the date.
- **Golden tests** on a real feed (owner action, §10), recorded with `scripts/record-ics-fixture.ts`, which fetches a feed the owner passes on stdin (never as an argument, so it stays out of shell history), keeps structure, UIDs, dates and course labels, replaces assignment titles with fixed fakes where the owner asks, and writes `src/core/todo/__fixtures__/elms-<date>.ics`. The script prints nothing about the URL.

- **As built** (`v3/ics-parser`): `parseIcs(text, source = "elms")` → `IcsParse` `{recognized, items: FeedItem[], skipped}` (`src/core/schema/ics-feed.ts`). A `FeedItem` carries `uid`, `source`, `title`, `courseLabel`, `courseCodes` (all of them; the server stores the first), `sectionCode`, `kind` with `kindFrom` (`uid` when Canvas's UID says; `title` when a dropped file makes us guess from words like "Homework" or "Lab"), `dueAt`, `dueDate`, `endAt` and `link` (ELMS hosts only). Events sharing a UID become one item (higher `SEQUENCE`, else the later one); `STATUS:CANCELLED` events are left out; a recurring event is read as its first occurrence. A floating time (no `TZID`) is read in New York; a `TZID` Intl can't place (or Outlook's Windows names, which are mapped) makes the event unreadable. Times in the hour daylight time skips or repeats follow RFC 5545 §3.3.5. Until owner action 2, the golden feeds are synthetic (`src/core/todo/__fixtures__/README.md`); `scripts/record-ics-fixture.ts` redacts a real one (the calendar's name, descriptions, locations, personal-calendar titles and user ids; `--fake-titles` for every title).

### 3.7 Calendar files

Terpsicle **never stores an ELMS password, never automates a login, and never fetches anything but the ELMS feed**. Gradescope detection (a tag on items whose ELMS entry mentioned gradescope.com, and a note about extensions) was removed in `v3/todo-calendar`: the owner expected a direct Gradescope connection, which isn't possible, and asked that Todo leave Gradescope out entirely (`docs/decisions.md`). Gradescope work a professor links in ELMS still comes through the feed like any assignment.

- **Drop a file.** On `/todo/connect`, "Add a calendar file" takes an `.ics` the student exported themselves. It's parsed **in the browser** with the same `parseIcs`; only the structured items (UID, title, course label, dates, kind) go to `todo/import-file`, which stores them with `source = 'file'`, skipping any UID the ELMS feed already has. A new file replaces the previous file's items. At most 2 MB and 1,000 items. Items are marked "From a file", and there's no server fetch for them, so they don't update: the page says "Files don't update. Drop a new one when your deadlines change."
- The file's raw text never leaves the browser.

### 3.8 API (`src/server/todo/`, all `auth: "user"`)

| Endpoint | Input → result |
|---|---|
| `todo/connect` | `{url}` → `{status: "connected", feed, items}` \| `{status: "invalid-link"}` \| `{status: "unreachable", reason}` \| `{status: "not-a-calendar", reason}` |
| `todo/disconnect` | `{}` → `{status: "disconnected"}` |
| `todo/list` | `{from: IsoDate, to: IsoDate}` (≤ 120 days) → `{feed: TodoFeedState \| null, items: TodoItem[], done: string[]}`; records `last_opened_at` (at most one write an hour) |
| `todo/done` | `{uid, done: boolean}` → `{status: "ok"}` |
| `todo/refresh` | `{}` → `{status: "fetched" \| "too-soon" \| "failed", feed}` |
| `todo/import-file` | `{items: TodoFileItem[]}` (≤ 1,000) → `{status: "imported", added, skipped}` |
| `todo/save-task` | `{uid, title, courseCode, dueDate, dueTime}` → `{status: "saved", item}` \| `{status: "too-many"}` \| `{status: "out-of-range"}` (§3.10) |
| `todo/delete-task` | `{uid}` → `{status: "ok"}` (§3.10) |
| `todo/hide-course` | `{key, hidden: boolean}` → `{status: "ok"}` (§3.11); `todo/list` answers `hidden: string[]` too |

- `TodoFeedState` = `{source, status, lastSuccessAt, lastFetchAt, lastError, itemCount}`. There's no field that could hold the URL, and a worker test asserts no answer from any `todo/*` route contains the fixture feed's token.
- `TODO_ENABLED` (var): `off` → every route `unavailable` and Todo leaves the product menu; `on`. Previews: `on` with test mode.
- **As built** (`v3/todo-api`): `todo/connect` answers `unreachable` for a timeout, a network error or a 5xx, and `not-a-calendar` for everything else (a 4xx, a redirect off ELMS, a page, too big). Since `fix/elms-connect-reasons`, both carry a `reason`: the fetcher's code (`timeout`, `network`, `bad-redirect`, `too-large`, `http-<status>`), or `not-recognized` when a 200's body isn't a calendar (`TodoConnectReasonSchema`). A failed call to our own server reads "You've been signed out. Sign in again to connect ELMS." on a 401, and "That didn't go through…" otherwise. `todo/list`, `todo/refresh` and `todo/done` work for file items without a feed; `todo/done` ignores a UID the person has no item for; `todo/refresh` on a `broken` feed answers `failed` without fetching. A fetch error that isn't in §3.5's list is `key` (a link whose key was removed before the cron resealed it). `/api/me`'s `flags.todo` is on when `TODO_ENABLED` is `on` and either `TODO_FEED_KEY` is set or the request is in test mode. Test mode answers three fixed tokens (`TEST_FEED_TOKENS` in `src/core/todo/test-feed.ts`: a calendar dated from today, a 404, a web page) from the Worker instead of the network, under the fixed key id `test`. The admin health numbers are `todoHealth()` in `src/server/todo/store.ts`, for `v2/admin-shell` to show.
- The client keeps done marks optimistic and keeps the last list in memory only. Nothing from Todo is written to IndexedDB in the first release: the data is the server's, and a shared computer shouldn't keep a student's deadlines.

## 4. Notifications: "Due tomorrow"

"Not too many notifications": V2 has four types and said don't add more. **`todo-due` is the owner's one approved exception**, with these limits:

| Type | Push | Email | Default | Sent when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `todo-due` | ✓ | — | **off until ELMS is connected**; connecting turns push on | At 6pm New York time, if at least one item due tomorrow isn't done. **At most one a day.** |

- **Settings:** `NotificationSettingsSchema` gains `todoDue: z.object({ push: z.boolean() }).default({ push: false })`, an additive field on the JSON column (no version bump; a stored row without it reads as off). `todo/connect` sets it to `true` the first time a feed connects; the person can turn it off. `/settings/notifications` shows the row only while a feed is connected; otherwise it says "Connect ELMS in Todo to get this."
- **Push only.** No email channel in the first release: one reminder a day is the whole promise, and email would double it for people with both.
- **Sending** rides on the Todo cron (§3.5): the first run at or after 18:00 America/New_York (computed in core from the run's time, so daylight saving needs no second cron) sends to each user with `todoDue.push` on, an `active` feed whose last success is under 26 hours old (stale data mustn't remind), and at least one not-done item with `due_date` = tomorrow. Runs until 23:59 catch up if one fails.
- **At most one a day** is enforced by `notification_deliveries.dedupe_key` = `todo-due:<userId>:<YYYY-MM-DD>` (unique), whatever retries happen.
- **Payload** (`PushPayloadSchema`, V2 §6.4): `{type: "todo-due", title: "3 things due tomorrow", body: "Project 2 (CMSC216) 11:59pm, WebAssign 5 (MATH240) 11:59pm and 1 more", url: "/todo?day=2026-09-29", tag: "todo-due"}`; `TTL` 21600, `Urgency: normal`, `Topic: todo-due`. One item: "Project 2 is due tomorrow" / "CMSC216 · 11:59pm". Titles are cut to 60 characters.
- **The `notification_deliveries.type` CHECK** gains `'todo-due'`. V2 §6.3's `0006_notifications` hasn't landed, so this document adds it there; if 0006 lands first without it, `0011_todo` rebuilds that table with the wider CHECK.
- Push needs the PWA on iOS (V2 §3.3). Turning on the row runs the same permission flow as any channel toggle.
- **As built** (`v3/todo-notify`; `src/core/todo/due-tomorrow.ts`, `src/server/todo/due-tomorrow.ts`):
  - **When:** each Todo cron run (`3,23,43 * * * *`), after its fetches, reads its own time on New York's clock (`dueTomorrowRun`, core's hand-kept US rule, so no second cron at the daylight switch): before 18:00 it sends nothing; from 18:00 to 23:59 it sends. So 6:03pm's run sends, and 6:23pm through 11:43pm's catch up anyone it missed. "Today" and "tomorrow" are New York dates, never UTC's (11:43pm EDT is already the next day in UTC).
  - **Who:** an `active` user with `todoDue.push` on, at least one device with push on, an `active` feed whose last success is under 26 hours old, no `todo-due:<user>:<New York date>` delivery yet, and at least one not-done item (feed or file) due tomorrow. Up to 2,000 people a run, 8 at a time; the next run takes the rest (people with no device are never picked, so they can't fill a batch). Sent through `notify`, and the key's `…:push` row keeps it to one a day. A push every device refused is claimed as `failed` and isn't tried again that night: the catch-up is for runs that didn't happen.
  - **The words** follow the payload above: the first two by due time (all-day items first, with no time) and "and N more", or one item's title with its course and time ("Due tomorrow" when it has neither). Titles are cut to 60 characters.
  - **Turned on at connect:** `todo/connect` turns it on when the person had no feed; pasting a new link over a connected feed leaves their choice alone. Disconnecting and connecting again counts as connecting, so it comes back on. "Due tomorrow" on keeps the feed on the 20-minute cadence and never paused (§3.5), in the cron and in `todo/refresh`.
  - **Settings:** `notifications/settings` answers `todoConnected` (a `todo_feeds` row exists); until then the row stays listed with its switch off and quiet (rather than hidden), and "Connect ELMS in Todo to get this." links to `/todo/connect` (a plain link, like the rest of the settings pages). Turning the reminder on wakes a feed paused while it was off. Connecting writes the setting only after the feed is stored.
  - The run's counts gain `dueTomorrow`, `dueTomorrowSent` and `dueTomorrowUnsent` (in the job's report, not in `todo_fetch_run`); a failure here is reported and doesn't fail the fetches.

## 5. Security and privacy

### 5.1 The feed link is a secret
- **Never logged**, anywhere: not in `console.*`, not in error messages, not in analytics, not in `notification_deliveries`, not in a URL or query string. It travels in exactly two places: the `todo/connect` POST body, and `crypto.ts` → the fetcher.
- **A worker test** connects a fixture feed, runs a failing and a succeeding fetch, disconnects, and asserts that the token appears in no captured log line, no server analytics event, no API answer, and no D1 column except `url_enc` (checked by scanning every table).
- **A lint rule** in `scripts/check-imports.ts`: only `src/server/todo/crypto.ts` and `fetch.ts` may reference `url_enc` or call the decrypt function.
- **SSRF:** only the two ELMS hosts, re-checked on every redirect (§3.5).
- **CSRF and origin:** every `todo/*` route is a same-origin POST with the origin check (V2 §12).
- **Rendering:** item titles and course labels come from professors and ELMS; they render as plain text. Links render only when they're on the two ELMS hosts, with `rel="noopener noreferrer"`.

### 5.2 Plan data
- The transcript paste never leaves the browser and is never saved; only the resulting doc is (§2.10, "What an import keeps").
- Grades: §2.5.
- Four-year docs are sync docs: stored like plans, deleted with the account.

### 5.3 `/privacy` updates
Each product PR updates its section of `/privacy` (built by `v2/privacy`), in plain words with contractions:
- **Plan:** "Your four-year plan is saved in your browser. If you're signed in, it's also saved on our server so it's on your other devices. That includes any grades you imported. Nobody else can see your plan or your grades, and we don't use them for anything else. ## 9. Later

### 9.1 Requirements from the catalog (a sketch, not v3's first release)

The owner's idea: a model reads UMD's prose requirements into code requirements, with "Report a problem", kept up to date by agents researching each major. Degree audit stays UMD's (u.achieve); this would be an unofficial checklist that says so.

- **Source:** the Undergraduate Catalog (academiccatalog.umd.edu, CourseLeaf), about 150 program pages. Each has requirement tables (`sc_courselist`: course rows, hour columns, area headers, "or" rows) and prose rules ("select five 400-level courses from at least three areas, no more than three in one"). A new ingest source, `src/ingest/programs/`, saves each page and extracts tables and prose with golden tests, like every parser.
- **A requirement language** in `src/core/schema/requirements.ts`, small and closed: `all`, `choose {n | credits}`, `course`, `wildcard` (the same `WildcardSchema`), `gen-ed`, and constraints (`maxFrom`, `distinctAreas`, `minLevel`, `notDoubleCounted`). Evaluating it is pure: `evaluateRequirements(spec, doc, index)` → per requirement `met | partly | not-met` with the entries that count.
- **The model** turns tables plus prose into that language, in JSON mode, validated by the schema; anything it can't express stays as the catalog's sentence, shown verbatim and never evaluated. Its output is LLM output, so the checklist carries the sparkles icon and "Read from the catalog by a model. Report a problem."
- **Review before publishing:** each program's spec lands in an admin queue as a diff against the last one; the owner publishes it. Published specs go to an R2 family `requirements/<program>/<catalogYear>.<hash>.json`.
- **Keeping it current:** an agent job per program re-reads the page each catalog year and when "Report a problem" reports pile up, and proposes a new spec to the same queue.
- **UI:** a Requirements tab in Plan, per program and catalog year ("Computer Science B.S., 2026–27"), always with "Unofficial. Your degree audit is the official check."
- **Out even then:** majors' special cases the language can't say (CS areas and concentrations, SOCY research courses) show as the catalog's text until someone models them by hand.
